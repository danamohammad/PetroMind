How PetroMind works

  1. Enter component mole fractions, critical properties and binary interaction parameters.
  2. Choose PR, PR78 or SRK. Thermo’s FlashVL solves vapor–liquid equilibrium at your temperature and pressure.
  3. Check phase fugacity agreement and component balance before accepting results.
  4. Flash the reservoir liquid through the separator and stock tank. Compute Bo and Rs on the resulting stock-tank oil basis.
  5. Sweep pressure and export the calculated points, inputs and diagnostics.

Scientific Python runs in a background browser worker through Pyodide. The first calculation downloads the numerical libraries. Your inputs are not sent to an analysis server.

What the results mean

Bo · RB/STB
Reservoir liquid volume divided by stock-tank oil volume.
Rs · SCF/STB
Gas liberated from reservoir liquid through the two-stage train, divided by stock-tank oil volume. Free reservoir gas is excluded.
Bg · RB/SCF
Reservoir gas volume divided by its single-phase gas volume at your selected reference conditions, with unit conversion.
Vapor fraction
Molar fraction, not volume percent.
Liquid compressibility · 1/psi
Fixed-composition isothermal volume derivative, shown only where adjacent states are liquid.

Scope and limitations

  • Research calculations, not field-validated engineering design.
  • One liquid phase and one vapor phase. No water/brine, solids, wax or asphaltene model.
  • The supplied C7+, C11+, C15+ and C20+ properties are uncalibrated. Their names are treated as separate cuts; verify they do not overlap in your source assay.
  • No volume translation. Liquid densities may need laboratory calibration.
  • Independent saturation roots are not a complete continuation algorithm. Near-critical and retrograde behavior may remain unresolved.
  • No fabricated quality contours, critical points or confidence intervals.
  • PR-derived default interactions are retained for model comparison; SRK may require its own fitted parameters.
  • No generative AI or API key is required. This release does not claim an AI-trained prediction model.

Changes from the supplied prototype

  • Rebuilt the crowded version-stacked interface into separate workflows.
  • Added explicit composition normalization, editable inputs, project save/restore and structured exports.
  • Enabled laboratory comparison for heavy pseudocomponent fluids.
  • Separated solver failure, unavailable properties and valid zero values.
  • Added stale-result tracking so changing inputs cannot silently relabel old curves.
  • Excluded secrets, virtual environments and IDE state from publication.
  • Replaced Windows-only startup assumptions with a browser runtime.
