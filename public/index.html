How PetroMind works
  1. Enter component mole fractions, critical properties and binary interaction parameters.
  2. Choose PR, PR78 or SRK. Thermo’s FlashVL solves vapor–liquid equilibrium at your temperature and pressure.
  3. Check phase fugacity agreement and component balance before accepting results.
  4. Flash the reservoir liquid through the separator and stock tank. Compute Bo and Rs on the resulting stock-tank oil basis.
  5. Sweep pressure and export the calculated points, inputs and diagnostics.
Scientific Python runs in a background browser worker through Pyodide. The first calculation downloads the numerical libraries. Your inputs are not sent to an analysis server.
What the results mean
Bo · RB/STB
Reservoir liquid volume divided by stock-tank oil volume.
Rs · SCF/STB
Gas liberated from reservoir liquid through the two-stage train, divided by stock-tank oil volume. Free reservoir gas is excluded.
Bg · RB/SCF
Reservoir gas volume divided by its single-phase gas volume at your selected reference conditions, with unit conversion.
Vapor fraction
Molar fraction, not volume percent.
Liquid compressibility · 1/psi
Fixed-composition isothermal volume derivative, shown only where adjacent states are liquid.
Model coverage and evidence
  • Calibration applies to the named sample, selected EOS, measured properties and tested conditions. Imported records show fitting and held-out errors. Field-design validation requires independent engineering review and operational evidence.
  • Hydrocarbon equilibrium currently includes one liquid and one vapor phase. Brine & solids adds separate NaCl density/viscosity, ideal multisolid wax equilibrium, and binary Flory–Huggins asphaltene phase separation. The modules do not form a coupled water–vapor–liquid–solid flash. Published examples and their validation domains are shown with each study. Flow assurance retains empirical pressure-trend and onset-reference tools.
  • Heavy fractions are editable. The public example retains assumed properties; calibrated projects can define distinct assay cuts and retain their source mapping.
  • Component-linear volume translation is available: V = V(EOS) − Σ xᵢ[cᵢ + cᵢ,T(T − 298.15 K)] in both phases. The optional temperature slope requires multi-temperature density evidence. It changes density and volume outputs without moving phase equilibrium. A zero shift preserves the original EOS. Calibrate shifts against appropriate density data.
  • Sequential differential liberation and multi-stage separators preserve gas-removal history and component balance. Laboratory protocols state their gas and residual-oil reference conditions. LBC oil/gas viscosity uses separate critical-volume inputs and supports fitting.
  • Adaptive continuation follows connected V–L boundaries through temperature and pressure turns. Criticality is checked by mixture stability equations. Failed or truncated traces remain explicitly partial; no disconnected-branch search or quality contours are claimed.
  • Automatic tuning is a bounded local fit. Rank, convergence and bound diagnostics remain visible. Validation and derived observations are excluded from fitting. Confidence intervals require a defensible measurement-error model; they are not inferred from correlated report columns.
  • Measured-data reference curves use shape-preserving interpolation inside one isothermal laboratory experiment. They are labeled separately from EOS predictions and never extrapolate.
  • Changing PR/PR78/SRK invalidates the calibration status. Fit and validate each EOS separately. Calculations use numerical thermodynamics; no API key is required.
